### #2 Public Opinion Quarterly (POQ) — HIGHER PAYOFF, LOWER PROBABILITY
- **Tier:** Top field — premier public-opinion outlet
- **Fit score:** 9/10 (intellectual fit) but lower acceptance probability
- **Acceptance rate (approx.):** 10-15%
- **Rationale:** The strongest intellectual fit but a meaningfully harder gate. The paper's central contribution — that bilateral warmth and issue-specific blame structure tool-configuration preferences in domain-bounded ways — is fundamentally a *public-opinion* finding. POQ is the one journal where the eight-probe specificity test (a measurement-design innovation in disguise) and the explicit acquiescence controls will be read as features rather than overhead. The 2x YouGov matched sample with documented weighting, MoE reporting, verbatim wording, and within-wave replication is exactly what POQ referees expect. The IBC framework is appropriately mid-level (cognitive-attitudinal, not formal IR theory), which fits POQ's house style. **Trade-off:** higher placement value if accepted, but a single-country observational paper without an experimental complement faces real desk-reject risk; POQ also takes longer to decide (~10-16 weeks).

## Honest Assessment

- **Strengths the paper plays to:** rigorous YouGov sampling and weighting; eight-probe specificity test as a built-in falsification design; conceptual contribution (IBC) is appropriately scoped — neither over-claims a formal theory nor under-claims a finding; sophisticated handling of acquiescence; substantive timeliness.
- **Weaknesses that may surface:** observational/cross-sectional (cannot causally identify blame → preferences); single bilateral case; no formal model; partisan-heterogeneity power limitations; SC1/SC2 scope conditions correlated empirically (acknowledged); domain-specific social desirability cannot be fully ruled out.
- **Top-3 desk-reject risks:** (1) IO/AJPS/APSR editors deciding the contribution is "narrow" or "policy-y" — hence not recommended; (2) reviewers asking for cross-national comparison or experimental complement; (3) "why not Chicago Council/Pew?" — addressed in §3 but a recurring concern.
- **If first choice rejects, the natural step-down tier is:** Chinese Journal of International Politics (CJIP) or Journal of Conflict Resolution (JCR) if framed around cooperation/coercion; International Studies Quarterly (ISQ) is a viable lateral move from FPA. Avoid stepping down to a generalist policy outlet — the methodological investment deserves a peer-reviewed audience.

## What NOT to Try

- **International Organization (IO):** Demands strong IR-theory contribution and cross-national or formal-theoretic scope. Single-country observational survey work without a formal model or audience-cost-style identification is high desk-reject risk.
- **AJPS / APSR:** Top-general bar requires a discipline-wide contribution and either causal identification or methodological innovation at the AJPS frontier. The paper's contribution is real but subfield-scoped — not the right gamble at current 85/100.
- **International Security:** Wants concrete decision-maker-facing policy implications and mixed-methods/qualitative depth. The paper's quantitative public-opinion focus is mismatched with its house style.
